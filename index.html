Why a CSE major, MatSci minor

The paradigm behind the path

Materials science runs on one loop: how you process a material sets its structure, structure sets its properties, and properties set its performance. CSE gave me the tools to model and automate every step of that loop — so I stayed in systems engineering and pulled materials science in alongside it, instead of switching majors.

01

Processing

Sputtering, PVD, additive manufacturing, DOE design — the physical inputs I control in the lab.

→
02

Structure

XRD, SEM, EBSD, AFM — reading the atomic and microstructural result of that processing.

→
03

Property

Mechanical, electrochemical, and optical behavior measured through testing and characterization.

→
04

Performance

Python/R models — Weibull, regression, Random Forest, Bayesian — that turn all of the above into a process recommendation.

CSE is the thread that runs underneath every stage: the code that designs the DOE, the models that close the loop from structure back to processing, and eventually the embedded systems that could run that loop autonomously. That's the engineer I'm training to be — not a materials scientist instead of a systems engineer, but a systems engineer who happens to work on materials.
